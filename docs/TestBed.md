# Test bed

> **Status (Oct 29, 2026): this page is partly out of date** ([#162](https://github.com/joelnl74/Hydro/issues/162)). `StatueVillage.hyscene` (and `Villagers.hyscene`) were deleted; the project now has `ShrineGlade`, `FarmVillage` and `Battle`. The sections about the village, the East Meadow, the Slime Field and the MVP scene describe scenes that no longer exist and are kept until they are rewritten for the field scenes. `Sandbox.hyproj` starts in `ShrineGlade` (Oct 29; a project whose start scene is missing now opens its first scene with a warning). The scene builders `scripts/shrine_glade/` and `scripts/farm_village/` referenced below are not in the repository. Scripts are now registered under their class names (`SpinScript`, `DioramaWalkScript`, ...; see [Script reflection](SceneAndECS.md#script-reflection-hy_script--hy_property-oct-28-2026)). The current scenes are in [Shrine Glade](#shrine-glade) and [Farm Village](#farm-village).

The **StatueVillage** scene (`Oxygen/project/Assets/Scenes/StatueVillage.hyscene`, the Sandbox project's start scene) is the engine's gameplay test bed. The 3D renderer's test bed is the [Shrine Glade](#shrine-glade), a separate scene that mixes meshes and sprites, with the [Farm Village](#farm-village) below it (the shrine's south path leads down to it). It is a small playable game, not a separate test area: every new engine feature is added to it as part of the game (a building gets a collider, the player gets a body, a plaza gets a trigger zone, a meadow gets pushable crates), so a feature is checked in the place where it will be used, together with everything else, and nothing falls out of date.

This page lists what the scene contains, how to interact with each feature, and what to check. The rules for adding to it are in [Scene, ECS and systems](SceneAndECS.md#test-bed-scene). This page is `docs/TestBed.md` and is mirrored to the wiki; it describes the scene as of October 25, 2026.

The map has three parts: the **village** (x -30 to 30, the original scene), the **East Meadow** (x 30 to 66), reached through the East Gate, and the **Slime Field** (x 66 to 96), where the road ends in a dirt arena and slimes roam. Together the map is 126 x 36 world units. Touching a slime or a spirit wisp starts a party battle in a second scene, `Battle.hyscene`; see [Slime Field and battle](#slime-field-and-battle).

The test bed is growing into the engine's first full test project, the **Diorama RPG** ([plan](DioramaRPG.md)): the player is **Orin** (warrior), **Lila** (white mage) follows him, and the art has been regraded towards the diorama palette (mossy grass, warm roads and cobbles, terracotta roofs, warm highlights and cool shadows, a darker forest edge around the map). The new art and the scene edits come from `scripts/diorama_rpg/build.py`; `preview_scene.py` next to it draws an offline mockup of a scene.

## Quick start

1. Build and run **Oxygen**; the Sandbox project opens `StatueVillage`.
2. Press **Play** in the toolbar (click the **Game** window if the keys do not respond).
3. Walk with **WASD** or the arrows. To reach the East Meadow, follow the road east out of the plaza and through the gap in the fence at x = 31. **Stop** returns to the editor scene untouched.

## Controls (play mode)

| Keys | What it does | Script |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **W A S D** or arrows | Walk Orin (the `Player`); plays `Walk<Direction>` / `Idle<Direction>`. Lila follows along his path | `VillagerWalk`; `PartyFollow` on `Party Lila` |
| **-** / **=** | Zoom the game camera out / in (8 to 36 units visible vertically) | `VillageCamera` |
| **V** | Top-down <-> diorama view: the camera eases to a 50 degree tilt and the trees, buildings, props and characters stand up and face it (also in the MVP scene; the view is kept through a battle) | `VillageCamera`, `CameraFollow` (`DioramaView`, `DioramaCamera.h`) |
| **O** | In the diorama view: perspective <-> orthographic | `VillageCamera`, `CameraFollow` |
| **1** / **2** / **3** | Day, dusk, night (global light and how strongly lamps and windows burn); the game starts at dusk | `VillageDirector` |
| **L** | All point lights on / off | `VillageDirector` |
| **H** | Blob shadows on / off | `VillageDirector` |
| **F** | Flame flicker on / off | `VillageDirector` |
| **B** | Statue <-> brazier A/B check | `VillageDirector` |
| **E** | Drops a crate in front of the player (at most 10; the oldest disappears) | `VillageCrateSpawner` on `Crate Spawner` |
| **1** to **6** in a battle | Attack, skill, skill, guard, potion, run for the hero whose turn it is; **Left / Right** (A / D) pick the target, **Up / Down** (W / S) set the boost. Only in the battle scene | `Battle` on `Battle Director` |
| **[** / **]** | Point light radius smaller / larger | `VillageDirector` |
| **F11** (or **Full screen** in the Game window's menu bar) | The Game window fills the editor and, by default, the whole monitor; **F11** or **Escape** leaves. Stop leaves too. Both are options in the Game window's **Options** menu and under Editor Settings > Play mode | Oxygen (`EditorLayer`) |
| **P** | Pause / resume the scenery animations (pond, torches: the animators no script drives) through `SpriteAnimator::Pause` / `Resume` | `VillageDirector` |

There is no in-game text: the Director's status line goes to the **Console** window, and the **Stats** window shows lit views, light and shadow counts and GPU time.

In the editor (not playing): click an entity in the **Hierarchy** or the **Editor** window to inspect it; every component, including the physics ones, is editable. Type in the Hierarchy's search box to filter rows by name; double-click a row (or press **F** over the Editor window) to move the scene camera to the entity. The selected entity is outlined in orange, and **Overlays > Collider Outlines** in the Editor window draws every collider (yellow sensor, green static, blue dynamic, cyan kinematic).

**Textures and sprites (the Finale pack, Oct 26, 2026)**: the pack's pictures came in `ShrineGlade/Finale/` (`Tiles`, `Tree`, `Props`, `Grass`, `Flowers`); since the Oct 30 restructure they are in `Sprites/Trees`, `Sprites/Plants`, `Sprites/Props` and `Textures/Ground` (the tiles renamed `ShrineDirt`, `ShrineMeadow`, `ShrineWallMoss`, `ShrinePathA/B`) and `scripts/shrine_glade/finale.py` turns the ones the scene uses into game assets (`SPRITES` and `TILES` name them). **Tiles** (Grass, Dirt, Flagstones, StoneWall, StepSlab, Boulder): the pictures do not wrap (their edges differ four to sixteen times more than neighbouring columns), so each is shrunk to one texel per art pixel (the pixel grid is 6 to 13 image pixels), made seamless by a minimum error cut (the picture and a copy shifted by half its width are joined along the path where they differ least, then the same the other way) and tiled at **32 texels per metre** like the sprites (the steps at 40): a flagstone is about 0.75 m, a wall block 0.5 m, a repeat 4 to 10 m. ## Interiors

`Assets/Scenes/FarmVillageInn.hyscene` (Oct 30, 2026) is the first **interior**: the Farm Village inn's common room, a scene of its own on top of the village. Since the same day the inn has an **upstairs**, `FarmVillageInnUpstairs.hyscene`: the guest rooms with the beds, a scene of its own on top of the common room (the bed that stood in the common room is gone). Both are built by `scripts/interiors/build_inn.py` (rerun it to regenerate; close the scenes first), **placeholders** of built-in boxes, cylinders and stairs with the medieval kit's textures (`Assets/Materials/Interior/Inn_*.hymat`, 32 texels per metre) until the inn interior kit is modelled in Blender (the brief: `art_incoming/Inn-Interior-Art-Prompt.md`). The field UI (inventory, dialogue, prompt, fade) is added by `scripts/ui/build_game_ui.py field`, as in the field scenes.

**How interiors work** (`MvpScripts.h`, `ShrineScripts.h`):

- A door is an interactable tagged `Door <Name>`. **E** fades out and pushes the scene `<this scene><Name>` (the Farm Village's `Door Inn` opens `FarmVillageInn`, so every village can have its own inn), or `<Name>` when there is no such file. A door with no scene behind it rests the party, as the inn's door did before (and warns once in the Console).
- `Door Out` in the interior fades out and pops back: the village is exactly as it was left, the player at the door. Played on its own (not entered from a field scene), the interior's `Door Out` only warns.
- **Floors are scenes too.** The common room's stairs (west wall, 14 risers over 3.4 m, 3.0 m up) are walkable and end on a landing; `Door Upstairs` there ("E  Go upstairs") pushes `FarmVillageInnUpstairs` on top of the common room, and upstairs `Door Out` at the stair head pops back, so the player is on the landing again. No spawn points are needed: each floor's player starts where the stairs arrive. A third floor or a cellar is the same pattern (`Door Cellar` opens `<scene>Cellar`).
- `Rest <anything>` rests the party (a dip to black, HP and MP restored), wherever it stands.
- The party's items are shared by every scene of a run (`MvpDirectorScript::s_Items`): what is used in the inn is used outside. The first scene of a run starts them afresh.
- The room is built at the characters' scale (the village's houses are bigger outside than their rooms are inside, as in Octopath Traveler), with the camera-facing (south) wall left out, a low sill and an unseen collider along the open front, and nothing around it: the project's black clear colour shows. The camera comes closer than the field's (`OrthographicSize` 7, the village's is 12) and the framing is the `InteriorScript`'s (tilt 52, lens 30, bounds within the room).
- Light (`InteriorScript`, render profiles `Assets/RenderProfiles/Interiors/Day`, `Evening`, `Night`: dim ambient, no fog, bloom): no sun and no sun shadows. `Light Window *` point lights take the preset's sun colour (daylight, evening amber, moonlight), `Light Fire *` lights (the hearth, candles) flicker like the field's lanterns. The interior starts in the time of day of the scene it was entered from (`DioramaDirectorScript::s_LastPreset`); 1 / 2 / 3 change it, L the fire.
- Characters behind furniture are talked to through a point in front of it: `Npc Innkeeper` is an empty entity at the bar's front (the line of sight from Miko's chest to a figure behind a 1 m counter would hit it); `Innkeeper` (the barmaid's sheet) is the sprite behind the bar. `Npc Patron` / `Patron` (the gatherer's sheet) the same by the table.
